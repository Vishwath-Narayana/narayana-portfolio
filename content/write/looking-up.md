---
title: Looking up
date: 2026-10-02
tag: Sky
summary: Why I want to learn the stars, and what a telescope has in common with a camera.
---
I like knowing what is up there. Not in a textbook way. I like the idea that I can point a telescope at a small dark patch of sky and see something that was invisible a minute ago.

Photography taught me to pay attention to light. Astronomy is the same habit, pushed as far as it goes. The light from a star left it long before I was born, and I am only catching it now. A photograph of a street is a record of a moment. A photograph of a star is a record of a very old one.

The settings are familiar too. Aperture, shutter speed and ISO still decide everything, only the numbers change. A shutter that stays open for seconds, a lens that is as wide and as fast as you can get, a tripod that does not move. It is the same triangle I use at dusk on a rooftop, with the dial turned to the extreme.

I have a lot to learn, and that is most of the appeal. I want to learn the constellations first, then the planets, then how to find a faint object without a map. After that, how to photograph it.

I will write here as I go. If you know the sky well, I would like to hear what you wish you had known first.
