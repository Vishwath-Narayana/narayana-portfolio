A new model lands every few months now, and most of the noise is about benchmarks. I care less about the scores and more about what people do with a model the week it arrives.

Opus 5.5 is the latest from Anthropic. The pattern with models at this level is that the interesting work does not come from the announcement. It comes from people who take it and point it at something stubborn: a codebase nobody wants to touch, a pile of documents, a task with twenty steps that used to need a person watching.

What stands out to me is how much of this is about agents and not chat. A model that can hold a long task, use tools, check its own work and come back with something finished is a different kind of tool from one that answers questions. The things people share are rarely clever prompts. They are working systems.

I come at this as someone who builds data pipelines and web products. For me the question is practical. Does it make the boring parts of engineering faster, and does it keep the person in charge of the result? So far my answer is cautious optimism. It is good at drafting and at grinding through detail. Judgement still has to come from somewhere else.

I will keep a list of the best things I see people make with it, and add my own experiments when they are worth showing. If you have built something with it, send it over.
